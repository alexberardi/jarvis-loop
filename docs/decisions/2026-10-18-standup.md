# The daily standup and questions to @orchestrator (2026-10-18)

Item #76, piece 6 of [the orchestrator](../phases/08-orchestrator.md): "a daily standup in
`#loop-digest` at 09:00 … (replacing the 4-hourly digest's item list)" and "a question to it starts
an orchestrator turn that answers in the thread". Pieces 2 and 3 had loopd record a message to
`@orchestrator` and start a turn, and loopd's `POST /orchestrator/replies` post an answer, but the
turn could not reach that route and did not know where the message came from.

## Decision

**Questions are answered in their thread by the turn.**

- The `message` trigger carries the message's channel, ts, thread and whether the approver wrote it
  (`pipeline.Trigger`); the turn's prompt prints them on the trigger's line ("from the approver;
  reply with channel `C…`, thread_ts `…`"). A reply goes in the thread the message was in, or the
  one it starts (`Trigger.ReplyThread`).
- The runner has two more actions: `reply` (channel, thread_ts, text: the replies route, posted by
  the orchestrator's own app) and `ask_approval` (an item held for approval, or waiting on a
  trusted-file OK: its message posted again with its button). The replies route now takes the
  `reason` every action carries and records it on the `orchestrator.reply` event.
- The skill: every message gets exactly one reply; only the approver's words make it act, within
  its authority; beyond it (a drop, an approval, an OK), a recommendation or `ask_approval`.
- **Not built here:** a turn acting for the approver beyond its authority (the API's
  `X-Loop-On-Behalf`, which the Slack side and scenario 59-orchestrator-acts-for-user use). The
  runner's actions have no on-behalf form yet; the approver's "drop #61" gets a recommendation in
  the thread and the Drop button stays theirs. A follow-up item if the user wants it.

**The standup is composed by loopd, not the model.** At 09:00 New York (`slack.StandupHour`, the
bot's `Location`), the loop bot's tick builds it from the store, the same way as the digest, and
posts it in `#loop-digest` through the `@orchestrator` app when loopd runs it (the loop bot
otherwise). No model call: it costs nothing, says the same thing every time for the same board, and
works when the orchestrator is capped or off.

- **The window is the 24 hours before 09:00** ("merged yesterday" means merged since yesterday's
  09:00, not the calendar day), so nothing falls between two standups.
- **Sections:** merged in the window; in flight (open items in a work stage waiting on nothing,
  with their stage, "working" when claimed, and the time since the item last changed); waiting
  (each unmerged dependency followed to its root, `pipeline.WaitChains`, as on the board); needs
  you (needs_you with its question and the orchestrator's open recommendation, held with the hold
  reason, qa, and open escalations about no item); what it did (the window's events by an
  orchestrator actor, counted by kind, each but notes and replies listed with its reason; refusals
  too); its spend (the window's turns against the daily cap, claude's cost and tokens). An item is
  in one of in flight, waiting and needs you; a merged item waiting for the user's check is in
  both merged and needs you.
- **Spend is recorded on the turn's event**, not in a new table: the runner sends claude's
  `total_cost_usd` and tokens (input with cache reads and writes, and output; both attempts when a
  result was refused once) with the turn's result, and loopd puts them on `orchestrate_done` /
  `orchestrate_failed`. #199 (usage awareness, still in build) adds a usage ledger keyed on item
  advances, which orchestrate turns do not make; when it lands, these events can feed it.
- **Once a day, no migration:** each standup is a `standup` event (`data.for`, the 09:00 it is for;
  `data.posted`). The first run only starts the clock (posted false), like the digest; a standup
  Slack refuses is tried on the next ticks and skipped after three (`digestTries`).

**The digest keeps its window and its status lines, and counts the items instead of listing
them** ("merged 1 · held 1 · needs you 2 · new 3"). It still lists main's CI not green (that cannot
wait for the morning) and keeps the builders line, the active agents and the last `/loop usage`.
The "waiting for your check" list moved to the standup's needs-you section.
