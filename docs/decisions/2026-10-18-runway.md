# Runway: warn before the loop runs dry (2026-10-18)

Item #205. Overnight 2026-10-17/18 the loop ran dry at 02:15 and sat idle until 08:04: every chain
waited on something of the user's. The orchestrator noticed at 02:10, but only as a generic
escalation.

## Decision

**The runway** (`pipeline.ComputeRunway`) is the work the loop can do without the user:

- **Runnable**: an item in a work stage (triage, build, review, land), claimed or not, whose
  dependencies are merged (qa or done, `DependencyMet`) or are themselves runnable and not on the
  hold list. An item in a work stage with risk hold (triage's risk hold, a hold-list path, a review's
  needs_review) and no approval for its current head (`RunwayNode.Hold`) is runnable itself, and
  its own stages count in the hours, but it stops at land for the user, so it meets no dependency:
  its dependents are not runway until it is approved for its head or merged. It is not listed as an
  unblocker either: until it reaches held there is no head the user could approve from the warning
  (an Approve button is bound to a head SHA); once held it is listed like any held item. An unclaimed
  item no runner can serve (`unservable`) is not. A qa item blocks nothing: dependencies are met at
  merge (2026-10-18-deps-at-merge.md), so the ask's "QA items that block dependents" does not arise.
- **Hours** are agent-hours: each runnable item's remaining work stages (its own and the ones after,
  through land) at the stage's median run time over its last 20 finished runs (leases that ended
  `advanced` or `queued`). With fewer than 3 such runs a stage takes a default (triage 10 min,
  build 45, review 15, land 10). #201's flow panel was not merged when this was built; its numbers
  can replace these medians later.
- **Unblockers** are the user's waiting items: held (approve), needs_you on a trusted-file OK
  (trust), other needs_you (answer). `frees` is how many items would become runnable if that one
  went back to work, **itself included**; an item behind two of the user's items is freed by
  neither alone. Ranked by frees, then priority, then id.

**The warning.** loopd's minute tick (`OrchTick` → `runwayCheck`) records one `runway_low` event
when the runway is below the control's `runway_warn_hours` (default 3, 0 off, at most 48), the user
is away, there is something to unblock, and no warning went out in this dry spell. Away: no event
of the user's (actor `slack:` or `user:`, not one the orchestrator took for them) and no message
of the approver's to the orchestrator for 30 minutes, or past 22:00 New York (`OrchDayZone`). The
dry spell is `orch_state.runway_warned` (migration 0023; it was 0022 alongside 0022_land_limit, so it never ran): set by the warning, cleared once the
runway is back at the threshold; a stopped loop or 0 active agents counts as off. It is loopd's,
not an orchestrate turn: it needs no model call and does not count against the turns cap.

**The message** is posted by the loop bot, not the @orchestrator app: its Approve merge and Trust
this commit buttons are the existing ones, handled by the existing handlers with the same approver
and head checks (an interaction goes to the app whose message it is). Their block_id is
`runway:<item>`, so pressing one leaves the warning (and the other unblockers) as it is instead of
replacing it with "approved". An answer has no button: the user replies in the item's thread.

**On demand.** The orchestrator's snapshot carries `runway`, and its skill says to answer "runway"
questions from it. The board shows a runway line (the top 3 unblockers). The standup's "before you
go" line waits for the standup (#76).
